### Changed — one page frame: header, content and footer line up on every screen

The header, the page content and the footer each had their own width, so on a
wide monitor they started at three different edges (at 1920 px: 24, 621 and
509 px from the left), every page put its content somewhere else, and on short
pages such as "Sign in" the footer floated in the middle of a tall screen.
Every page now uses one frame:

- Header, content and footer share the frame (at most 1120 px wide, centred)
  and its side margins: 16 px on a phone, 24 px on a tablet, 32 px from 1024 px.
- The footer always sits at the bottom of the screen; no page is centred
  vertically any more, so a tool page no longer jumps when its result appears,
  and every page starts the same distance below the header.
- Content columns come in four fixed widths instead of seven: 672 px for tools
  and legal pages (legal pages were 624 px), 448 px for sign-in forms, 896 px
  for the catalogues and the account page (were 848 and 720 px), and the whole
  frame, 1056 px, for pricing and the admin cockpit (were 976 and 1104 px).
- Footer links are 44-px rows on a phone and 24-px rows from 640 px (WCAG 2.2
  SC 2.5.8), in 14 px instead of 12 px text; the mobile menu and its language
  links are 44 px high.
- The cookie bar no longer covers the footer's legal links on narrow phones:
  it stays at the bottom of the screen while you scroll and takes its own
  place above the footer at the end of the page.
- Native controls (select lists, date pickers, scrollbars) are drawn dark
  like the page; printed pages stay light.
- Page headings grow with the screen, from 26 px on a phone to 36 px on a
  large monitor, and stay at about 30 px on a laptop so the tool is still in
  the first screen.

Texts, links and addresses are unchanged. Under the hood, every page's `<main>`
comes from the `page()` macro, the shell's colours come from design tokens,
and `tests/test_layout_standards.py` keeps hand-built frames and
screen-height centring out.
